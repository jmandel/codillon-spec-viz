10 — Synthetic proof holes: goals as inserted lines

Codillon already writes lines you did not type: when a frame is unclosed it inserts a synthetic end, drawn as a dashed darkred box with 𝄠 in the gutter. This prototype does the same for proofs. When an obligation at line L cannot be discharged, the editor inserts a hole row right after L. The hole is the goal drawn as a fraction, the same glyph Codillon uses for stack types: hypotheses above the rule (i32 shapes holding values, with the facts known about them), the goal below (split into conjuncts). Next to the fraction sit up to three suggestion chips. The rule says what kind of hole it is. It is broken and darkred when a counterexample exists, and the counterexample values sit inside the shapes, ready to replay. It is dashed indigo with ★? when the checker merely cannot decide. Accepting a chip turns the hole into a real line: the box solidifies, flies to where the edit belongs, and the line reveals itself. Editing the code so the goal holds makes the hole vanish. Holes are read-only and never take the cursor. The reasoning failure becomes part of the program text, in reading order.

A — the primitive in isolation

Four tiny functions. Hover any chip to preview its edit; click it to accept. Click a hole body to see that holes refuse edits (shake). A1 and A2 share one program, so a spec change in A1 can move a hole into A2.

Self-critique

  • What it makes obvious. Where the reasoning breaks, in reading order, as a row of the program. False and unknown look different in shape (broken vs. dashed rule, ⚖ vs. ★?) as well as colour, and the hole always says what would close it. The chips put the "fix the code or fix the spec?" choice (principle 13, Clousot-style) right at the goal, and accepting one shows the consequence: a hole that moves (A1→A2, (b)→delete, (d)→termination) is the clearest teaching moment here.
  • What it hides. The hole sits at the failing obligation, which is not always where the fix goes (S1's gt_s at 17, P1's line 15). The dashed edit-site outline and the darkred trail carry the blame, but they are secondary marks. A hole also says nothing about how often things go wrong over time (the slider and memory panel carry that). Proven-with-search (the hash table's counting lemma) is drawn as plain proven.
  • How it scales. One hole is 3–4 rows tall, so ten holes would push a 100-line function far apart. Mitigations prototyped here: a hole reports the first failure per root cause and folds its echoes into an "also needed by / same goal at" line, folded functions auto-open only when they contain a hole, and the residue list mirrors the gutter. Not prototyped: collapsing a hole to a one-row strip when it is not focused.
  • Cost in Codillon. Low on the rendering side: holes reuse synthetic_before, the reveal/shake classes and the fraction SVG renderer, plus one new row kind. The chip previews reuse the editor's own line diffing. The real cost is the checker: a residue with per-conjunct status, witness kind (input vs. loop-head state), unsat-core sources for the trails, and a ranked suggestion engine (invariant strengthening, lemma apply? by conclusion shape, requires-tightening by weakest precondition). The engine's line interpreter is ~250 lines of JS and reproduces the fixture.
  • Deviations. Listings follow 03's line numbers and fixture traces with 01's syntax ($-names, named results, (@use …)). The hash table keeps 03's (@define …) lines verbatim. Lines that holes write ((@use sq_lt_sq $r 46341)) are numbered 15′ so fixture numbering stays fixed. Student-path stage ① blanks lines 7–8, following the fixture's convention for deletions. Long header lines are set smaller than 22px. Folding is not in Codillon today. The "hole after the probe loop" for (b) sits at $find's early return (line 41), where the goal actually lives.
