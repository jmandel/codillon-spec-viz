02 · Wraparound ring with twin ticks — overflow made spatial

WebAssembly integers have no sign: the operator chooses (gt_s or gt_u). So the i32 value space is drawn as a ring, not a line. 0 sits at the bottom, non-negative values climb the right side, negative values the left, and the two meet at a seam at the top, where 2147483647 + 1 wraps to −2147483648. The unsigned reading has its own seam at the bottom (−1 is 4294967295). Every refined value gets a small ring in the right margin. Its admitted set is a shaded arc closed by fence posts (filled = inclusive, hollow = exclusive). The runtime value is a bead with twin ticks, its signed and its unsigned reading. A spec term such as (r+1)*(r+1) lives in ℤ, so it is drawn as an unrolled arc that can spiral past the seam, and the bead lands wherever that arc wraps to. Overflow is the bead jumping the seam. A comparison operator cuts the ring at its seam (signed at the top, unsigned at the bottom), and a predicate is faithful only if its ℤ arc does not cross the cut. The 8-slot hash table is also a ring (index & 7), and its probe sequence is an arc on that ring. The scale is a fisheye: it is magnified near 0 and near the seam, the two places where i32 arithmetic surprises people.

Self-critique

  • What the ring makes obvious. Overflow and signedness. On a number line they are invisible; here they are two places: the top seam (signed) and the bottom seam (unsigned). In S1 nothing has to be read. The comparison's cut swings from the bottom to the top of the ring (line 17), the product's ℤ arc now crosses it, and the seam flashes on line 15, which is where the wrap happened. S2 is a fence post that turns from filled to hollow with the bead sitting on it, best seen in the loupe. Bug (a) is the mask made literal: the unmasked index leaves the table ring through the seam where & 7 would have wrapped it. Dependent bounds visibly move with their anchor: √n hangs from n's bead.
  • What it hides. Relations among more than two values, quantified facts, and memory beyond one table. The hash table's R clauses still need conjunct chips and the table ring, because a single i32 ring cannot show them. Parity becomes a symbolic comb, because the fisheye cannot show individual teeth. The fisheye also cannot separate small integers, so I added a loupe: a linear integer strip under any big ring whose values of interest fall within about 200 consecutive integers. Without it, S2 and S3 would be hairlines.
  • Scale. On the 169-line table, rings appear only on the 14 lines that carry an obligation or a refined value. The gutter keeps ⚖ / 💣 / ✶ / ○. A gutter minimap beside the scroller marks ⚖, 💣, refuted rings and the arrow, so a bug outside the scrolled view still shows within a glance. Mini rings are 22 px, so they only say "calm or not"; the two lens rings and the table ring carry the detail. The 695k-step S1 real run is a dashed ghost track, not a replay.
  • Kind discrimination. The six states differ in line style and glyph, not hue alone. Proven is a quiet filled band. Refuted and replayed is a solid dark bead, a hatched spill, a seam flash and a solid ⚖. Refuted but not replayed is a hollow dashed bead, a dashed spill and a dashed-circle ⚖. Held-on-this-run is a dotted band with ∘N. Unknown is a dashed band with ✶. Vacuous is a 50% hollow ring with ○. Use the grayscale button to check. A 22-px mini ring in grayscale distinguishes only refuted from the rest; the lens and gutter carry the full distinction.
  • Implementation cost in Codillon. Moderate. A ring is one SVG <g> per refined value: arcs, posts, and a bead that is rotated, so the existing 0.25 s tweens move it around the ring and across the seam rather than through the middle. The fisheye map is five lines. The real cost is upstream: for every obligation the checker must report its reader (signed seam, unsigned seam or ℤ), the ℤ term and its machine value, and the anchor of each dependent fence. That is roughly the residue record of 01 §2.8 plus a reader field and the wrap-blame list.
  • Deviations.
    • Listing text and line numbers follow 03, with names n, r and v, because the fixture's edits are string replacements on those lines. In 01 syntax these would be $n, and the header is shown wrapped into func / param / result rows to suggest that.
    • The table has 8 slots, as in the fixture, not the 16 in idea 05.
    • The slider traces are full step logs regenerated with the fixture's own reference interpreter. They match every fixture key frame (line, stack and checks), and the S1 trace is exactly the fixture's loop-head replay.
    • 03's lemma obligations are shown as proven by default, the "nonlinear lemmas found" setting. The Checker menu in B shows the same obligations as held on this run or unknown.
    • In C the clean run is 03's correct-run sequence. Each bug loads its own witness sequence.
