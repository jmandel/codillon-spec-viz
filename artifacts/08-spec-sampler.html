08 · Spec sampler — relation plots and edge-case grids (coverage, not proof)

The spec is treated as an oracle and sampled: every executed call (your own run, edge probes such as 0, 1, −1, INT_MAX, INT_MIN, powers of two and perfect squares, and any solver witness) becomes a point drawn on the spec's own shape. A dependent result gets a small relation plot docked beside its function: input across, result up, the region the result refinement admits shaded in the type colour (the staircase v*v ≤ n < (v+1)² for isqrt). Points inside the shading are small ivory dots, points outside are darkred and clickable (they load that run into the slider). Multi-input and sequence specs become an edge-case grid. Everything you have not sampled stays blank, so one good run is one dot, not a proof. The samples sit below the proof verdict, carry a “sampled · not proof” label, and every spec line gets a thumbnail of its own samples in the right margin, so the darkred lands on the clause that failed.
