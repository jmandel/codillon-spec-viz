05Invariant horizon and lap chart on the loop frame

A loop's frame bracket gets a gate at the loop line: the place where the invariant is checked. A thin horizon line runs out from the gate. Every time control reaches the loop head, a tick lands on the horizon: the entry tick (▸) is drawn differently from back-edge ticks (lollipops). A lap chart hangs from the horizon, docked to the frame. It has one row per invariant conjunct and one row for the decreases measure (a staircase that must descend), and one column per lap executed up to the slider position. Two extra cells sit on the frame line itself: established? at the gate and ⇒ postcondition? at the exit. Where a failure lands in the chart tells you what kind it is. A failed entry (left column) means the invariant was never established. A failed cell at lap k means the invariant was not preserved. If every lap holds but the exit cell is unknown, the invariant is too weak. If the staircase goes flat, the loop does not terminate. Silence (ivory) is success.

Section BNumber theory: $isqrt and $gcd

Both programs come from the shared fixture, with one instruction per line. Each has its own step slider and seeded bugs. For a bug, the run selector switches between your run (the student's own test call) and the checker's ⚖ witness. An input witness is a full run from the function's entry. A loop-head witness is a one-lap replay that starts from an injected state at the gate (◇). It shows a counterexample to induction.
