# Review of Refined Codillon (`artifacts/00-refined-codillon.html`)

**Overall verdict.** `00-refined-codillon.html` is a serious, unusually cohesive piece of interaction and information design that succeeds at its hardest task: extending Winstein’s no-error-panel, continuous-execution WebAssembly editor to specification checking without turning the margin into a wall of theorem-prover text. The clean state is genuinely calm, the core visual primitives—the piecewise linear-log `i32` number-line shape, the darkred spill over an input cup’s rim, the inline synthetic proof hole (`𝄽`), and the on-demand integer ring and loop lap-chart lenses—earned their keep, and the live unified interpreter passes all 144 fixture key frames on load. Nonetheless, the page has several real defects that need fixing before it can stand as the definitive design specification: a factually inverted prose explanation and broken replay jump in Lesson 3’s Seed (a), a rendering bug where blamed fix-lines (`fixAlso`, such as line 17 `i32.gt_s` in Lesson 2) draw their fraction rule as *proven* and their cup spill as *unreplayed* even after replaying the counterexample, an overly narrow boundary test in the Memory Mirror lens that falsely labels `R` as `"open: mid-operation"` throughout read-only `$lookup` calls, visual collisions around multi-operand refined shapes, and several pedagogical leaps where a reader who knows WebAssembly but not Hoare logic or refinement types is left without the bridge they need.

---

## A. Tutorial clarity

A reader who knows WebAssembly (stack effects, two’s-complement `i32`, `gt_s` vs. `gt_u`, linear memory, traps) will grasp the basic premise—types as rectangles, refinements as shaded sub-regions, typechecking as spatial containment—within the first two sections. Where the tutorial stumbles is at the seams between ordinary Wasm execution and verification concepts (binder conventions, mathematical vs. machine arithmetic, inductive cut-points at loop heads, and causal trails).

1. **Unexplained shifts in refinement binder syntax (`$x` vs. `v` vs. `$e` vs. `v₇`).**
   - In §0, the first refinement a reader sees is `(func $half (param $x (i32 | $x % 2 == 0)) (result i32))`, accompanied by the sentence: *"The parameter now says `(i32 | $x % 2 == 0)`, so the shape of `$x` on line 2 grows from 30 to 84 px [72 px in `rv00-top.png`] and becomes a number line with a comb."*
   - Two sentences later, §1 ("A type is a set") switches without explanation to bare `v` and `n` without WAT `$` sigils: *"A refinement `(i32 | P)` carves out the region where `P` holds"*, with picker buttons `(i32 | 0 <= v)`, `(i32 | 0 <= v < n)`, and `(i32 | v == 2*x)`.
   - In §2, the listing switches back to `$i` in `(param $i (i32 | 0 <= $i < 8))`, while the callout chips use `v` (`1 <= v < 9`) and the hole below line 8 introduces subscripted synthetic stack names (`v₇ < 8`, and later `v₁₅` in Lesson 2).
   - In §3, function headers introduce named result binders that do not exist in standard WAT (`(result $h (i32 | $h + $h == $x))`), and then in §5 (Lesson 3) the hash-table listing abruptly reverts to unnamed results and bare `v` even inside parameter refinements (`(param $i (i32 | 0 <= v && v < CAP))`).
   - Only at the bottom of the page, in §7 *Limits*, does the text admit: *"Mixed syntax. Lessons 1 and 2 use the 01 syntax (`$x` binders, named results). Lesson 3 shows the fixture’s hash-table text verbatim, which binds the result as `v`."* A Wasm programmer reading §§1–5 linearly needs one explicit sentence in §1 stating that `v` (or the parameter/result name) binds the value inhabiting the refined type, and that `v₇` / `v₁₅` in proof holes name the anonymous stack value produced on line 7 or 15.

2. **Contradictory setup in §1 ("Six states, one cup") and undefined "vacuous".**
   - §1 states: *"Below, the same cup requires `0 ≤ v < 8` and is fed `1 ≤ v ≤ 8`, so the value 8 spills over the rim. The six panels are six things the checker can say about that spill."*
   - To a careful reader, this setup is self-contradictory: if the producer’s set is `[1, 8]` and the consumer requires `[0, 8)`, then `[1, 8] ⊆ [0, 8)` is mathematically false; the checker could never validly report **proven** about that spill. In fact, `sixPanel()` in the JS secretly changes the provided set from `[1, 8]` to `[1, 7]` in the first panel (`rv00-s1.png`), so there is no spill in the `proven` panel despite the prose saying all six panels show the same cup and spill.
   - The sixth panel introduces **`assumed / vacuous`** (*"ghosted to 50%, hollow, `○`, and everything downstream is ghosted too"*), but the tutorial never defines what a *vacuous* obligation is (a check under contradictory assumptions or on an unreachable path) or why it shares a visual state with an explicit user `(@assume P)`.

3. **§2 ("Reading a line") explains the margin fraction with callouts, but leaves the proof hole and causal trails unexplained.**
   - §2 says: *"In Refined Codillon the fraction is a sequent. Facts known about the inputs sit above the rule, facts claimed about the outputs sit below it, and the rule itself is the verdict. ... the cup on line 8 spills, and the editor inserts a hole after it that holds the counterexample."*
   - While the 2× diagram on the right of `rv00-s2.png` labels six parts of the margin fraction (`provided set`, `facts above`, `claims below`, `required set`, `the rule is the verdict`, `spill`), it gives **zero callouts** to the much denser **proof hole** inserted below line 8. A newcomer is left to infer how to read the hole’s hypothesis row (`$i`, `v₇`), its full-width broken rule, its conjunct boxes (`0 <= v₇` with `0 ≤ 8` vs. underlined darkred `v₇ < 8` with `8 < 8`), and its right-hand suggestion chips.
   - Worse, the vertical dashed lines with dots in the far-left gutter of `rv00-s2.png` (connecting lines 5 and 7 to the hole) are **never mentioned anywhere in §§0–6**; a reader only learns what they are if they read the "Left out" bullet of §7 *Limits* (*"What survives of them is the causal trail: dashed lanes from a hole back to the lines its hypotheses came from, in darkred for the line to blame"*).

4. **§3 (Lesson 1 · parity) glosses over occurrence typing and mathematical vs. Wasm modulo.**
   - §3 says: *"Line 15 negates `is_even(x)`, and the `if` on line 16 sends odd numbers to the then-arm. In the else-arm `$x` is known to be even, so the comb on line 20 fits the cup on `call $half` (line 21) tooth for tooth."*
   - For a Wasm programmer, local `$x` was declared as an unrefined `(param $x i32)` on line 12, and `local.get $x` on line 13 has a plain 30×11 rectangle. Seeing `local.get $x` on line 20 suddenly produce an 84×14 comb rectangle (`cur-L1-clean.png`) is the central conceptual leap of refinement type systems (path-sensitive refinement / occurrence typing narrowing the type of a variable inside a branch), and the prose should explicitly contrast line 13’s unrefined shape with line 20’s refined shape.
   - Similarly, a Wasm programmer knows that Wasm’s signed remainder `i32.rem_s` gives `-3 % 2 == -1`, whereas line 12’s spec writes `$x % 2 == 1` even for negative inputs (`half_or_neg1(-3)`). The tutorial never states the ground rule from `03-sample-programs.md` §1: spec expressions operate over unbounded mathematical integers ($\mathbb{Z}$) with Euclidean modulo (`%`), not 32-bit wrapping arithmetic.

5. **§4 (Lesson 2 · `isqrt`) under-explains loop-head counterexamples (CTIs) and hides the lemma statement.**
   - Step 1 says: *"At the top of the loop the checker knows nothing about `$r`. It finds a loop-head state, n = 3 with r = 5, that leaves through the exit with a wrong answer."* A student who knows Wasm execution will immediately object: *"Line 3 initializes `$r` to 0, and for `n = 3` the loop exits at `r = 1`; how can `$r` be 5?"* Ten words (*"At the top of the loop the checker knows nothing about `$r`"*) is not enough to explain why verification cuts loops at their header and treats any modified local as arbitrary unless constrained by an `@invariant`.
   - Step 2 says: *"With r = 46340 the product on line 15 is 46341² = 2147488281."* It never mentions *why* `46340` appears: $\lfloor\sqrt{2^{31}-1}\rfloor = 46340$, so $r = 46340$ is the largest value admitted by the invariant $r^2 \le n \le 2^{31}-1$, making $(r+1)^2 = 46341^2$ the unique boundary case that crosses `INT_MAX`.
   - Step 3 says: *"The wrong instance shakes; `(@use sq_lt_sq $r 46341)` closes the hole."* Because the signature of `sq_lt_sq` (`0 <= x && 0 <= b && x*x < b*b ==> x < b`) is hidden inside a hover tooltip (`data-tip`), a reader who does not hover the button cannot tell what `sq_lt_sq` states or why `($r+1) 46341` is refused.

6. **§5 (Lesson 3) and §6 ("What the checker hands the editor") assume verification background.**
   - In §5, the prose discusses strengthening clause `(iii)` of `R` when lines 7–11 (`R0`–`R4`) are folded by default (`rv00-L3.png`); the reader never sees the text of clause `(iii)` (*walking from `h(k)` to `k`’s slot never crosses an `EMPTY` slot*) unless they click line 12 or hover the tiny `R(iii)` chip.
   - In §6, the table translates `(i32 | P)` to *"a subtype `{v : Int32 // P v}`; the stack is a Σ-telescope of them"* (`cur-s6.png`). Dropping "Σ-telescope" without a five-word gloss ("each slot’s type can name earlier slots") is unnecessary type-theory jargon in an otherwise accessible tutorial.

## B. Visual design

### What succeeds
- **Calmness of the proven state (`cur-L1-clean.png`, `cur-L2-proven.png`).** Banning green, keeping lenses closed until clicked, hiding proven fact chips until a line is hovered, and using a plain gray line number with a 1px rule means a verified module has virtually the same ink density as unrefined Codillon (`cur-s0.png`).
- **Piecewise number-line geometry and the spill glyph (`rv00-s1.png`, `rv00-L1.png`).** Allocating the middle 60% of the rectangle linearly to `[-8, 8]` and the outer 20% wings logarithmically to `INT_MIN` and `INT_MAX` makes both small constants (`0`, `1`, `8`) and overflow boundaries (`2¹⁶`, `2³¹−1`) legible in the same coordinate system. Drawing a failed subtyping check (`provided \ required`) as a darkred hatched spill rising 3px above the consumer cup’s rim—while turning the last 28% of the incoming stack curve dotted darkred—is completely faithful to Codillon’s spatial idiom.
- **The integer ring and loop lap-chart lenses (`rv00-L2.png`, `cur-L2-proven.png`, `cur-L3-dweak.png`).** In `rv00-L2.png`, drawing `i32` as a circle with `0` at the bottom, the signed seam (`INT_MAX | INT_MIN`) at the top, `i32.gt_s` as a red cut across the top seam, and `46341² = 2147488281` spiraling past the top seam to `-2147479015` makes signed wraparound immediately obvious. Likewise, prototype 05’s rule in the lap chart—cell *fill* from the run (ivory vs. darkred), cell *border* from the checker, plus the `⋯ 46 340 laps not run` hatch—cleanly separates "observed on this trace" from "proved for all traces."

### Where clutter, ambiguity, or visual bugs appear
1. **Blamed fix-lines (`fixAlso`) fail to update their cup spill, fraction rule, and fact chips (`rv00-L2-crop.png`, `cur-L2-signed.png`).**
   - Look closely at line 17 (`i32.gt_s`) in `rv00-L2-crop.png` and `cur-L2-signed.png`, where the S1 witness has been replayed:
     - The gutter on line 17 shows a **solid** darkred `⚖` (from `S.fix['L17']`).
     - Yet the spill on line 17’s first input cup is drawn as a **dashed hollow outline** (the *not-yet-replayed* `ghost` style, not the solid darkred hatch!),
     - Line 17’s fraction rule beneath the spilling cup is an **intact gray proven line** (not a snapped red rule!), and
     - Line 17’s refuted fact chip (`= s`) is **completely hidden** unless the user hovers line 17!
   - Why? Because `H-back` sets `at: 'L23'` and `fixAlso: 'L17'`, so `this.S.st['L17']` is `undefined`. In `fracSVG()` (line 1807), `rst` defaults to `'proven'`, which passes `status: 'proven'` to `shapeSVG()` (whose spill renderer treats any status other than `'refuted'`, `'unknown'`, `'dynamic'`, `'assumed'` as `'ghost'`) and passes `'proven'` to `ruleSVG()`, while `factsHTML()` only displays chips when `this.S.st[r.key]` is defined! This directly breaks the page’s visual contract on the most important line of Lesson 2.

2. **Micro-collisions inside and between margin shapes (`rv00-L2-crop.png`, `cur-L2-signed.png`, `cur-L2-nolemma.png`).**
   - In `rv00-L2-crop.png` (at `72 × 12` px) and even after the HTML increased refined shapes to `84 × 14` px (`cur-L2-signed.png`), line 17 (`i32.gt_s`) places cup 0 (`x = 6..90`) and cup 1 (`x = 98..128`) only 8px apart, while cup 0’s overflow spill nub extends `7.2 px` past its right wall (`x = 90..97.2`). In `rv00-L2-crop.png` the spill box collides with cup 1’s left border; in `cur-L2-signed.png` they clear by `< 1 px`.
   - On lines 15–17 of `rv00-L2-crop.png`, the Bézier curve from line 15’s output (`i32.mul`) to line 17’s cup 0 slices directly through line 16’s output shape (`local.get $n`) and its right-wall fence post.
   - On line 15 (`i32.mul`), the wrapped value dot sits on the far left wall (`-2147479015`), while the text `-2.15e9` is right-aligned inside the shaded `[1, INT_MAX]` region on the right half of the rectangle, making the negative number look like a label for the positive shaded region.

3. **Epistemic state discrepancies between the legend and the listings (`rv00-s1.png`, `cur-L2-nolemma.png`, `cur-s2-gray.png`).**
   - **"Held on this run only" (`dynamic`, state 4) is never shown as a standalone state in any lesson.** In Lesson 2 Tab 3 (`cur-L2-nolemma.png`), `∘ 4` appears only as a secondary annotation inside an `unknown` (indigo) hole. None of the three lessons has an obligation whose primary state is `dynamic` (slategray dotted cup, rule, and chip).
   - **Unknown lines lose their gutter glyph in the listing.** Both the §1 Six States card (`rv00-s1.png`) and the §7 table (`rv00-s7.png`) show `𝄽?` in the line’s gutter for the `unknown` state, and §7 states *"Proven is silence: a gray line number."* Yet in `View.fill()` (line 1744), `unknown` and `dynamic` lines are not given a gutter glyph at all: in `cur-L2-nolemma.png`, line 15 (`i32.mul`, which is `unknown`) has a plain gray line number `15` in the gutter, reserving `𝄽?` exclusively for the inserted hole row below it.
   - **Grayscale chip collision (`cur-s2-gray.png`).** In the §2 Vocabulary legend under `gray=1`, the chip border for `refuted, not replayed` (`1.2px dashed darkred`) and `unknown` (`1.2px dashed indigo`) have identical dash patterns and nearly identical grayscale luminance, distinguished only by a 9.5px trailing `?`.

4. **Left-gutter causal trails conflict with Codillon’s spatial grammar (`rv00-L2-crop.png`, `rv00-L3.png`).**
   - Up to four parallel dashed vertical lanes spaced 4px apart (`x = 6, 10, 14, 18 px`) run down the far-left margin from hypothesis lines into each hole. In upstream Codillon (`00-codillon-today.md` §5), vertical lines on the left of the instructions represent `block`/`loop`/`if` control-flow frames. Refined Codillon drops Codillon’s control-flow frame lines entirely and puts hypothesis trails in the left gutter instead, where long vertical runs (such as the 30-row trail from line 12 to line 41 in `rv00-L3.png`) look like structural block brackets.

5. **Lens affordances and small layout glitches (`cur-L2-proven.png`, `cur-L2-S2-rel.png`).**
   - Clickable function headers append a tiny `⋰` character to the code text (`.row.k-func.clickable .code::after`), which in `cur-L2-proven.png` line 1 looks like stray dust after `($root+1))) ⋰`, while clickable `loop` lines (`loop $grow`, `loop $probe`) have no visual affordance at all.
   - In the Relation Plot lens (`cur-L2-S2-rel.png`), the y-axis label `↑ result` collides with the top border of the hatched `n<0` exclusion bar, and the 41 sample dots (`r = 3.2–4 px` at `9.75 px` horizontal spacing) merge into a nearly solid bead string for `result = 5` and `6`.

## C. Correctness

Checking the prose and visual states against `03-sample-programs.md` and the embedded `fixture.json` reveals four concrete correctness errors:

1. **False claim in §5 and `H-a` about Seed (a)’s execution order (`💣` on line 142 vs. `⚖` on line 151), compounded by a broken ternary in `View.replay()`.**
   - **Prose (§5):** *"Seed (a) drops the mask on the probe index: when you replay it, the run hits 💣 at line 142 before it ever reaches the ⚖ on 151. A bomb outranks a scale."*
   - **Hole note (`H-a`):** *"a run reaches the load at 142 with address 65536 first: 💣 outranks ⚖ there"*
   - **Ground truth (`03-sample-programs.md` §B.7, bug `(a)`):** During `insert(15, 150)`, `h(15) = 7`. On the first probe (`j = 0, i = 7`), line 140 `call $slot(7)` returns `65524` and line 142 `i32.load` (step 139) loads `st(7) = 1` (FULL) **without trapping**. Control then enters `if` (line 143), computes unmasked `i = 7 + 1 = 8` on line 146, increments `j` to `1` on line 150, and **executes `br $free` on line 151 at step 143**, where the loop invariant `i == at(k, j)` (`8 == 0`) **fails dynamically (`⚖`) first**! Next, on the second iteration (`j = 1, i = 8`), line 140 `call $slot(8)` fails `$slot`’s precondition `0 <= i < CAP` at **step 145 (`⚖`)**, and only at **step 153** does line 142 `i32.load` trap with 💣 at address `65536`. You can see the two darkred failure ticks (steps 143 and 145) to the left of step 153 on the slider track in `cur-L3-a-tall.png`.
   - **Broken replay jump (`00-s5-arep.png`):** In `View.replay()` (line 1692), the code reads:
     ```js
     if (run.halted === 'trap') i = run.recs.length - 1 >= 0 && h.key !== 'H-a' ? i : i;
     ```
     Because both arms of `? i : i` return `i`, clicking `▸ replay` on Seed (a) jumps to `i` = step 143 (line 151 `br $free`), **not** step 153 (the trap on line 142). And because `View.paint()` only renders 💣 when `this.cur === run.recs.length - 1`, **clicking `▸ replay` on Seed (a) never shows a 💣 glyph anywhere (`00-s5-arep.png`)**! Even when the user manually scrubs to step 153 (`cur-L3-a-tall.png`), 💣 appears on line 142 (replacing line number `142`), while `⚖` is on lines 147 and 151—so 💣 and `⚖` never compete for the same gutter slot.
   - **Missing static spill on line 140:** In `03-sample-programs.md` §B.7, the static failure for Seed (a) is at **line 140 (`call $slot`, `pre of $slot: 0 <= i < CAP`)**, because `i` can be `8` on the second iteration. In `PROGS.hash.derive()`, `ann.L139` (`local.get $i`) is hardcoded to `outs: [RG.iv(0, 7)]` even when `f.code === 'a'`, so line 140 falsely shows `[0, 8)` fitting inside `[0, 8)` with no spill.

2. **The Memory Mirror lens falsely reports `"R open: mid-operation, R may be false"` at step 241 of Seed (b) (`rv00-L3.png`).**
   - In §5, the prose states: *"Seed (b) makes `$find` stop at a TOMB as if it were EMPTY. `R` stays true the whole time."*
   - Yet in `rv00-L3.png` (at step 241, `lookup:72 call $find (return)`, which is the exact step `▸ replay` jumps to for Seed (b)!), the Memory Mirror lens header reads **`R open: mid-operation, R may be false and is not checked here`** and grays out all five chips `R(o)`–`R(iv)`!

Because `mirrorLens()` (line 2266) defines `boundary` as `(run.boundaries || []).includes(r.s) || view.cur === run.recs.length - 1 && !run.halted`, where `run.boundaries` only records the final return step of each top-level harness call (`61, 170, 218, 247`), ignoring internal function boundaries (`$find` entry/exit, even though `$find` has `(@requires R)` on line 21) and ignoring the fact that `$lookup` and `$find` never mutate memory or `$M`.

## D. Soundness of the design itself

1. **Where "a refinement is a region inside the shape" is coherent—and where it breaks down.**
   - As a visual algebra, drawing `{v : T | P(v)}` inside the shape of `T` is genuinely coherent when `P` is a **unary subset of a 1D ordered domain**: intervals (`[a, b)`), one-sided bounds (`0 <= v`), small finite sets (`{0, 1, 2}`), and small-period congruences (`v % 2 == 0`).
   - It breaks down as soon as refinements become **relational, algebraic, or heap-dependent**:
     - *Relational stack/return types ($\Sigma$-telescopes):* Consider `(result $h (i32 | $h + $h == $x))` in Lesson 1, `v == gcd(a, b)` in `03-sample-programs.md` A3, or `(acc * pow(base, k)) % m == pow(b, e) % m` in A4. Statically (quantified over all inputs), `{h : i32 | ∃x. h + h == x}` is simply `[-2³⁰, 2³⁰−1]`, which erases the relational dependency on `$x`. At a single dynamic step where `$x = 6`, the slice `{h | h + h == 6}` collapses to the singleton `{3}`, which is indistinguishable from the runtime value dot itself. Revealingly, the page’s own Lesson 1 leaves the return shape of `$half` on lines 10 and 21 **unrefined** (`30 × 11` px) and puts `= x/2` into a text chip instead—silently conceding that relational postconditions live in the sequent chips, not in the number-line region.
     - *Non-interval arithmetic and bit-vectors:* Bitmasks (`(v & 0xF0) == 0`), large-modulus remainders (`pow_mod`), or floating-point predicates (`f32`/`f64` NaN/ulp bounds) cannot be represented legibly by fence posts, teeth in `[-8, 8]`, or sockets on an 84px bar.
     - *Memory and data-structure invariants:* A representation invariant like `R0 && R1 && R2 && R3 && R4` is a predicate on linear memory and ghost state, not a subset of any stack operand’s `i32` rectangle. In Lesson 3, the region-in-shape metaphor does almost none of the heavy lifting (only the `[0, 8)` slot index and `{0, 1, 2}` slot state use it); the actual specification story is carried by text chips `R(o)`–`R(iv)` and a bespoke circular SVG widget (the Memory Mirror). In a real editor, the checker cannot synthesize a bespoke circular hash-table diagram from arbitrary `forall s. load(BASE + 12*s) ...` formulas unless the specification language includes declarative view annotations.

2. **Where the design would break in a real CS 10N editor.**
   - **Local SMT counterexamples (CTIs) vs. whole-program replay traces.** The page’s strongest interactive device is that every darkred refutation has a `▸ replay` button that loads a concrete counterexample onto the time-travel slider. In a real SMT-backed verifier (Z3/cvc5 in Wasm), a failed verification condition produces a **local model at the VC boundary** (a counterexample-to-induction state at a loop head or function entry), **not** an executable multi-call trace from `main` like `insert(3,30) · insert(11,110) · delete(3) · lookup(11,-1)`. Synthesizing a 4-call operation sequence that reaches a violated internal assertion requires bounded model checking or property-based fuzzing in addition to modular SMT checking. Conversely, when the solver *does* hand back a local CTI (like `n = 3, r = 5` in Lesson 2 Tab 1), intro students accustomed to Codillon’s concrete execution slider will be baffled by why `r = 5` appears on the slider when line 3 set `r = 0`.
   - **Horizontal scaling for 2- and 3-operand instructions.** At `RW = 84 px` per refined shape, a 2-input instruction with two refined operands (`i32.gt_u` on line 17 of `isqrt`, `cur-L2-proven.png`) already consumes `6 + 84 + 8 + 84 = 182 px` of the `190 px` fraction column. A 3-operand instruction (`select`, `memory.fill`, or a `call` taking three refined parameters like `$pow_mod(b, e, m)` in `03-sample-programs.md` A4) requires `274 px`, colliding with the `.facts` chip column at `fracX + 196 px`.

3. **What Lean, Dafny, and Liquid Haskell users would expect that is missing.**
   - **Explicit framing (`modifies` / `reads`) for linear memory.** In Lesson 3, `$lookup` preserves `R` only because it performs no `i32.store`. In a real Wasm module (where audio/graphics buffers share linear memory with data structures), modular verification of `call $helper` is impossible without frame specifications (`(@modifies ...)` or memory regions) stating which addresses a call may overwrite.
